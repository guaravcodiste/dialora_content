SaaS growth has a hidden tax: support cost that scales just as fast as revenue.

The average SaaS support team fields 100 to 500+ tickets a day. Most are routine:

- "How do I reset my password?"
- "Where is last month's invoice?"
- "Can I change my billing email?"

Each one still takes a human 10 to 15 minutes: log in, verify identity, search docs, answer. Multiply that across a 50-person team spending 40% of its time on routine questions, and you have lost the equivalent of 20 people to work that should not need a human at all.

The math gets worse from there. Support agents cost $45,000 to $70,000 a year. Add 20 more to keep pace with growth and you are looking at $900,000 to $1.4M in new annual cost, just to answer questions your own documentation already covers.

A 50% increase in customers should raise support cost by 15 to 20%, not 50%. Instead, most SaaS companies end up with one support person per 100 to 150 customers, a structure that breaks the moment growth accelerates.

Enterprise customers make it harder. They expect phone support, not a four-hour email wait. Covering that around the clock takes 4 to 5 people per shift, meaning a 50-person team needs roughly 250 staff for true 24/7 coverage. Few SaaS companies can justify that math.

Misrouting adds another layer of waste. A billing question lands with a product specialist. A bug report lands with the newest hire. Resolution time climbs, agents get frustrated, and customers repeat themselves on a second call. What the ideal setup actually needs is simple:

- Answer simple questions immediately, no human required
- Route complex questions straight to the right specialist
- Keep full context through the entire customer journey
- Cut down repeat contacts through first-contact resolution
